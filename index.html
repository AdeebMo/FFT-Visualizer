1. What FFT Is

FFT is a fast way to compute the DFT

The Fast Fourier Transform, or FFT, is an algorithm that computes the Discrete Fourier Transform efficiently. In simple terms, it helps us move from data written as coefficients or time samples into a value or frequency view where some operations become much easier.

In this project, the main application is polynomial multiplication. We want students to see both why FFT is useful and how the butterfly computation creates the speedup.

2. Why We Need FFT

Direct multiplication becomes slow as polynomials grow

Start with something simple like (1 + 2x + 3x^2)(4 + 5x). Direct multiplication is easy to understand, but every term in one polynomial has to meet several terms in the other.

Many pairings: one coefficient interacts with many others.
Extra bookkeeping: equal powers of x must be collected back together.
Big-picture problem: this direct method gets expensive for large inputs.
3. Polynomial Representations

Coefficient form and value form tell the same story in different ways

Coefficient form A(x) = 3 + 2x + x^2 → [3, 2, 1]
Value form [A(x0), A(x1), A(x2), ...]

A polynomial can be represented either by its coefficients or by its values at selected points. Multiplication is harder in coefficient form, but much easier in value form because matching values can be multiplied point by point.

4. Evaluation And Interpolation

These two ideas connect the two representations

Evaluation means starting with coefficients and computing the polynomial at chosen points. Interpolation means starting with those values and reconstructing the coefficients.

  • FFT: fast evaluation at special points.
  • Inverse FFT: fast interpolation back to coefficient form.
  • Why it matters: polynomial multiplication needs both directions.
5. Roots Of Unity Intuition

FFT becomes fast because it evaluates at special points

FFT does not evaluate a polynomial at random points. It uses carefully chosen points called roots of unity. These points have symmetry, and that symmetry is what lets the algorithm split and recombine work efficiently.

Formal notation ωn = e-2πi / n
6. DFT Vs FFT

Same goal, different speed

DFT

The direct transform already computes the values we need, but it does so slowly.

FFT

FFT computes the same result more efficiently using divide and conquer.

O(n^2)

The direct DFT compares many outputs with many inputs and repeats work.

O(n log n)

FFT reduces the work by splitting into smaller transforms and reusing structure.

7. The Core FFT Idea

Split the input into even and odd parts, then combine later

FFT uses divide and conquer. Starting from a list like [a0, a1, a2, a3, a4, a5, a6, a7], it separates the coefficients into even-indexed terms and odd-indexed terms, solves the smaller transforms, and then combines them.

Even-indexed group: [a0, a2, a4, a6]
Odd-indexed group: [a1, a3, a5, a7]
Why it helps: repeated splitting reduces the total amount of work.

The next section focuses on the combine step, which is the butterfly operation you can explore interactively.

8. Butterfly Computation

This is the basic merge step that appears throughout FFT

After smaller FFT subproblems are solved, their results are merged with butterfly operations. A butterfly combines two values using addition, subtraction, and a twiddle factor. The interactive visualizer below lets you watch that pattern repeat across the circuit.

Inputs

Where the two values come from

Each butterfly receives one value from the upper branch and one from the lower branch. These values come from smaller FFT subproblems that have already been solved.

Twiddle Factor

Why one branch is multiplied

One input is multiplied by a twiddle factor W. This rotation supplies the phase information needed to combine the even and odd pieces correctly.

Butterfly formula u = a + Wb,   v = a - Wb
Outputs

How the pair moves into the next stage

The butterfly produces a top output and a bottom output. Those two updated values feed the next stage of the FFT and help build the final transformed list.

10. Inverse FFT

We need the inverse to come back from value form

Forward FFT evaluates a polynomial at special points. After multiplying corresponding values, inverse FFT reconstructs the coefficient list. This is the interpolation idea in algorithm form.

Forward FFT

Coefficient form to value form

The forward transform takes a coefficient list and computes its values at the chosen roots of unity. That is the fast evaluation step.

Inverse FFT

Value form back to coefficient form

After pointwise multiplication, inverse FFT brings the answer back into coefficient form. Without it, we would know the values of the product but not its final coefficients.

Pipeline

How FFT Multiplies Polynomials

In value form, multiplication becomes pointwise, and inverse FFT brings us back to coefficients.

  1. Start with both polynomials in coefficient form.
  2. Pad both arrays with zeros to a power-of-two length that can hold the product.
  3. Evaluate both padded arrays at roots of unity using FFT.
  4. Multiply the evaluated values pointwise.
  5. Use inverse FFT to return to coefficient form.

A small example keeps the pipeline concrete

13. Complexity

Why FFT is fast

The main advantage of FFT is efficiency. Divide and conquer plus butterfly reuse changes the scaling in a major way for large inputs.

Direct Method

O(n^2)

Direct multiplication or direct DFT work grows quadratically because many terms are compared against many others.

FFT-Based Method

O(n log n)

FFT reduces the work by splitting the problem, reusing smaller results, and combining them with structured butterflies.

14. Common Misconceptions

Quick corrections that help students self-check

These are the ideas that often cause confusion when students first learn FFT.

FFT is not only for audio

It is also useful for convolution, polynomial multiplication, image processing, and many other computational tasks.

FFT does not multiply polynomials directly

It first changes the representation, then multiplication becomes easy in value form.

A butterfly is not the whole FFT

The butterfly is only the basic merge unit that repeats throughout the full algorithm.

Inverse FFT is necessary

Without it, the result stays in value form and cannot be read as the final coefficient list.

Evaluation and interpolation are different

Evaluation moves from coefficients to values, while interpolation reconstructs coefficients from those values.

Summary

Key takeaways from the whole lesson

FFT is a fast method for evaluating a polynomial at special points. It works by repeatedly splitting the input, solving smaller transforms, and combining them with butterfly operations.

What the algorithm is doing

FFT reorganizes the DFT into smaller pieces that can be solved and merged efficiently.

Why it matters for multiplication

For polynomial multiplication, FFT transforms both polynomials into value form, multiplies corresponding values, and uses inverse FFT to recover the final coefficients.
